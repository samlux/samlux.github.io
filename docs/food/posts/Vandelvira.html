Vandelvira: ‘Genius cuisine’’

Fine Dining
Spain
Published

September 11, 2026

Baeza, a quaint city about half an hour’s drive north of Jaén, is home to Chef Juan Carlos García’s family-owned convent. In 2022, he reopened this historic establishment into a restaurant, and his culinary creations have been nothing short of remarkable. The food here reminded me of Reale *** in Italy, and it’s no coincidence that Chef García has been collaborating with the renowned chef Niko Romito this year to explore the complexities of bitterness. I want to use the term ‘genius cuisine’ to describe their food, a feeling that I did not get anywhere else, not noma, not Plénitude.

Chef García’s dishes are bold, innovative, and occasionally challenging, but in a delightful way. The service is impeccable, in sync, yet infused with warmth and genuine hospitality. I firmly believe that this restaurant is on a rapid ascent towards earning more Michelin stars. Here are a few of my personal favorites from my recent visit (my absolute favorites are 1, 4, 5, 6, 8, 11):

P1 Squid and Iberian ham: thinly sliced squid and ham with ham dashi. The texture of squid and ham complements with each other, and the fat from ham enhanced the squid flavor. The dashi is full of aging flavors.

P2 Chard, licorice beurre blanc: this is the first of three licorice courses, with the strongest licorice flavor. I personally do not like licorice that much, but the contrast of sweet and bitterness from chard is very interesting.

P3 Green peppers, caviar and licorice: this one is more complex with acidity from pickling the pepper, a slight spiciness from pepper, savory from both pickling and caviar, and the sweetness from licorice. I prefer this one to the previous one because it’s more balanced.

P4 Shrimp and licorice: this one is my favorite one among the three. The shrimp is cooked tableside with 90oC sea water pouring on it. The added sweet liquor to the head of shrimp reminded me a classical combination of Shaoxing wine and raw shrimp in Chinese cuisine.

P5 White Asparagus: the pickled asparagus is bright, refreshing, and crisp. Great transition to the heavier dishes.

P6 White anchovy and anchovy sauce: the sauce is creamy and full of umami flavor. The texture of the white anchovy is insane: I thought it was cured extensively because of its gelatinous texture, however, they told me it was only cured in salt for 6 minutes.

P7 kaki and trout: another fascinating combination between Hachiya persimmon and trout roe. The sweetness and a hint of tanginess from the fruit really highlighted the savory and ocean flavor in the trout roe. My only critic is that they put too much roe.

P8 Green peas and “butifarra”: I really like the complex pork fatty flavor and texture from this Catalan sausage. I think they might also blend in sticky rice. The added truffle is from a local mountain where the farmer started to spread truffle spore 16 years ago and only be able to start harvesting recently.

P9 Rabbit: rabbit skewer on rosemary and rabbit brain. Great semi glaze.

P10 Black mole and blood sausage: another very complex dish with spiciness, bitterness, savory. It tastes like putting a churro without sugar or cinnamon into Mexican black bean stew.

P11 White radish, ham toffee, and Maritozzo: I’m not sure if this is mean to be a desert, but if it is, then it’s my favorite desert for the recent years! The radish is so tender and absorbed all flavors from the ham, kind of like a high-end Japanese Oden. The Iberian ham and truffle work very well with the radish.

P12 Caramelized egg custard and peppers: interesting texture with a bit raw egg taste.

P13 Piquillo peppers, black pepper and chocolate: It’s not my first time to have pepper with chocolate, but it still surprised me.

P14 Canelé, chocolate and ginger: great canelé, firm outside, soft inside.
